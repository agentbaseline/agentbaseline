Security outcomes for enterprise AI agents

Draft for public comment · v1.0-draft · 30 July 2026
PDF · controls.yaml · repository

Agents are arriving in your organization on someone else's timeline. Security is often not in the room when that decision is made, and by the time it is, agents already hold access to real data and real systems. You can allow adoption without controls you trust, or you can block it and become the obstacle — while it continues in the shadows anyway.

Your existing controls still do their job. They were built for software whose behaviour is settled before it ships. An agent's is not: an end user reprograms it at runtime, in natural language, changing what it does and which approved tools and data it reaches for. The enterprise is provisioning a programmable actor, and it can act at machine speed before a person can intervene.

Buying the gap closed is its own problem. “Agent governance” can mean almost anything. Every product solves some part of this; few will tell you which part, and no datasheet explains how the parts fit together.

So judge by outcome rather than by product category. Six outcomes, because together they answer the three questions you are already being asked:

  1. What is operating, and under whose authority? DiscoverAuthorize
  2. Is it staying inside approved boundaries? ConstrainValidate
  3. Can you prove what happened — and stop it? ObserveRespond

No single enforcement point delivers all six. They are logically one system and physically scattered across identity, runtime, tooling, data and evidence — which is what makes the list useful. Take it into a vendor conversation and ask which of the six they cover.

The six outcomes

  1. DiscoverDIS-01…07 Discover every agent and its dependencies.
  2. AuthorizeAUT-01…06 Bind identity, task, target and authority.
  3. ConstrainCON-01…07 Limit components, runtime, data and reach.
  4. ValidateVAL-01…05 Admit the system and verify outcomes.
  5. ObserveOBS-01…04 Correlate activity and prove what happened.
  6. RespondRES-01…04 Stop, revoke, quarantine and scope impact.

33 controls across the six. By this test, none of the three organizations that convened this document is a complete implementation. Use it on us.

What this is

A draft, open for comment until 30 September 2026. It is not a standard, and no conformance programme exists. Nothing here certifies anyone, including the people who wrote it.

Identifiers are not yet frozen. At v1.0 they freeze, and from then on a control is superseded rather than renumbered — so a citation you publish keeps meaning what it meant. Read VERSIONING before you cite.

Contributing

Tell us where a control is wrong, unimplementable, or missing — against a specific identifier, so the argument has somewhere to land.

The contribution we need most is the one we cannot write ourselves: a crosswalk to a framework you already run — NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, OWASP, CIS. Everyone who drafted this works for a vendor in the space, so a mapping from outside is worth more than another control from inside. See CONTRIBUTING.
