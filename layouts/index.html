Agent Baseline

  • Identify the agent.
  • Bound its authority.
  • Control its actions.
  • Prove its outcomes.

Agents arrive on someone else's timeline. Security is often not in the room when that decision is made, and by the time it is, they already hold access to real data and real systems. You can allow that without controls you trust, or block it and become the obstacle while it continues in the shadows anyway.

Your existing controls still do their job: they were built for software whose behaviour is settled before it ships. An agent's is not. An end user reprograms it at runtime, in natural language, changing what it does and which approved tools and data it reaches for. You are provisioning a programmable actor. It reaches sensitive data, calls tools and acts at machine speed, so a mistake or a malicious instruction becomes a harmful action before a person can intervene. Buying the gap closed is its own problem: “agent governance” can mean almost anything, and no datasheet explains how the parts fit together.

A category tells you what a product is called. An outcome tells you what has to be true. There are six, and between them they answer three questions:

  1. What is operating, and under whose authority? DiscoverAuthorize
  2. Is it staying inside approved boundaries? ConstrainValidate
  3. Can you prove what happened — and stop it? ObserveRespond

No single enforcement point delivers all six. They are one system, scattered across identity, runtime, tooling, data and evidence.

The six outcomes are a category test, not a certification scheme. A product may cover one or more; an enterprise architecture is complete only when all six are addressed. Take them into a vendor conversation and ask which they cover.

What this is

Written for the security, platform and architecture engineers who have to make a decision about agents. It extends the identity providers, policy engines and SIEM platforms you already run rather than replacing them, and it does not prescribe how a model reasons — only the conditions under which an agent, and each of its material actions, may proceed.

A draft, open for comment until {{ .Site.Params.commentsUntil }}. It is not a standard, and no conformance programme exists. Nothing here certifies anyone, including the people who wrote it.

Identifiers are not yet frozen. At v1.0 they freeze, and from then on a control is superseded rather than renumbered, so a citation you publish keeps meaning what it meant. Read VERSIONING before you cite, and the controls for the reference form.

Contributing

Tell us where a control is wrong, unimplementable, or missing. File it against a specific identifier, so the argument has somewhere to land.

The contribution we need most is the one we cannot write ourselves: a crosswalk to a framework you already run: NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, OWASP, CIS. Everyone who drafted this works for a vendor in the space, so a mapping from outside is worth more than another control from inside. See CONTRIBUTING.
